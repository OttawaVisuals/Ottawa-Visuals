What is in the approved scope?

$483.9M combined authority = main project + parking + retail. This is our sum of the three rounded authorizations. It excludes private tower construction and financing interest.

How the main project is financed

The City’s November 2025 financing plan. These rounded amounts sum to $418.9M, a $0.1M rounding difference from the main budget. City financing memo ↗

Borrowing and revenue forecasts are different. The financing plan includes $331M in additional debt. The City projects revenues to offset much of its cost over time; projected offsets are not cash already received.
Debt payments, parking and retail: what the headline leaves out

The City’s November 6, 2025 memo estimates annual debt payments of $17.4M on $331M of debt, assuming 4.25% interest over 40 years. It says the budget allocation ramps up to $17.4M by 2036. This is a planning estimate, not verified final borrowing terms.

The City projects $288.2M in revenue offsets and describes the remaining investment as approximately $130.7M. Its Q&A describes the funding figures as net present values: future amounts expressed in today’s dollars. These figures do not represent a cap on future costs or guaranteed revenues.

Parking has $19.2M in separate authority. Retail has $45.9M: the approval identifies $39.2M to be reimbursed by the partnership and $6.7M funded through air rights. Their financing differs from the main project.

Data gap: this page uses the July 21, 2026 spending memo. It is not a live ledger, a final project cost or a complete list of Ottawa’s major projects. Newer costs, financing terms and realized revenue need review before an update.

Sources, method & gaps

What the budget charts measure

Operating: the “Expense” column in the adopted book’s Operating Summary by Committee, printed pages 2–5 (PDF pages 21–24). It is net of internal recoveries and after interdepartmental allocations, but before each program’s revenue. It is not the property-tax levy. Water, wastewater and stormwater are rate-supported; tax-supported programs can also receive fees and grants.

Capital: new authority from the capital summary, printed pages 17–19 (PDF pages 36–38). Projects can span years. Existing authority from previous budgets and complete lifetime project costs are not included. Transit-related projects appear under several committees; the chart preserves those committee groupings.

Funding: operating revenue categories from printed page 1 (PDF page 20). Fees include water and other service charges. “Own funds” are internal funding, not new outside revenue. Capital has separate financing; this chart is not the funding mix for the entire city capital program.

2025 is the budget comparator printed in the 2026 adopted book, not 2025 actual spending. All changes are nominal, without inflation or population adjustment. Amounts are stored in $000; the charts round to millions. Service group labels are shortened; community services are split into social services and parks/recreation/facilities.

Denominators, rounding & why totals cannot be added

Shares divide a category by the selected view’s total. Filtering operating services changes the denominator. Comparison bars in share mode use each year’s own total. A larger share does not necessarily mean more dollars.

The operating overview uses the City’s printed totals: $5,218.430M for 2026 and $4,903.996M for 2025. Summing the rounded service rows gives $0.004M and $0.002M more, respectively. Capital committee rows sum to $0.001M less than the City’s 2026 total. No row is altered to hide these differences.

Operating expenses include contributions to reserves and debt charges that support capital. Adding operating expenses to new capital authority can double count internal funding and mix annual expenses with multiyear approvals. The accounting presentation also differs from the City’s full-accrual financial statements. This page therefore presents separate totals.

What this page does not establish

No ward-level allocation, household tax-bill estimate, audited 2026 actual spending or long historical series is provided. Capital authority is not a prediction of cash spent in the year. Budget growth alone does not establish improved services, waste or value for money.

Lansdowne financing, modelled net investment, capital authority and actual spending come from different dated documents. Their scopes and timeframes remain separate. The July 2026 memo is the spending snapshot used here, not a claim that no newer update exists.

Manual source review; no automatic refresh. The budget build script stops when expected rows disappear or totals fail reconciliation. Future updates require reviewing the source before publication.
