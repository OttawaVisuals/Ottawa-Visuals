Threshold definitions, exceptions and sources

Primary: classes containing grades 1–3, possibly with JK/K, but no grades 4–8. Count above 20 for the 90%-at-or-below-20 rule, and separately above the 23-student individual cap. Primary/upper mixed: classes with JK/K or grades 1–3 plus grades 4–8; compare with 23.

Kindergarten-only: count above 29 and above the exception ceiling of 32 separately. Up to 10% of eligible classes may have 30–32 students under specified conditions; exception fields are unavailable. A class above 29 is not automatically a breach.

ETFO proposals: 26 for individual JK/K-only classes and 24 for grades-4–8-only classes. The latter is not the provincial 24.5 board-average rule. No individual-class legal cap of 24.5 is applied.

The numeric thresholds are based on O. Reg. 132/12, sections 2–10, read alongside the 2025–26 reporting guide. Union proposals: ETFO 2025–26 funding submission, recommendations 14–15. No secondary-school screening is possible from this elementary dataset.

05 / Read the limits

What’s missing?

  • Secondary and private-school class sizes: no comparable school-level history established in this release. The elementary workbook covers publicly funded boards, including Catholic schools.
  • Early coverage: 2007–08 to 2013–14 files contain no grades-4–8-only classes. All-class and upper-grade histories therefore start in 2014–15.
  • Small cells: “<5” is interpreted as 1–4 because zero is separately published as 0. This assumes suppression applies to positive cells. Blank or unknown values are not converted to zero.
  • Special education and staffing: the public class file has no staffing, class-property or self-contained-special-education fields. These are reported class rows, not a complete measure of student needs or staff workload.
  • Geography: these are entire board territories, not students living within Ottawa’s municipal boundary. French-language boards serve a wider eastern Ontario region.

Ontario states that it does not fund private schools. The private-school spending line includes private institutions’ expenditure; it is not an Ontario government subsidy. Ministry explanation ↗

Sources, method & downloads

Official sources
  • StatCan 37-10-0066-01: Ontario public/private education expenditures, 1954–55 to 2023–24; source values in thousands of dollars are multiplied by 1,000. Public education includes school boards, direct government spending, federal schools and other public components.
  • StatCan 37-10-0067-01: financing by direct source. “Private funding” adds fees and other private-sector sources. It can finance public education; it does not identify private-school ownership.
  • StatCan 18-10-0005-01: Ontario all-items annual CPI. Purchasing-power adjustment uses the average of each school year’s two calendar-year indices, rebased to 2023–24. This is an approximate school-year consumer-price adjustment, not an education cost index. Available from 1979–80.
  • Ontario elementary class-size workbooks: class enrolment on a September instructional count date selected by each board. Historical workbook plus 2023–24, 2024–25 and 2025–26 releases. Only the English-labelled copy is imported; it includes French-language boards too.
  • 2025–26 reporting guide and StatCan private-school estimation note.
How class sizes are calculated

Each class row has six enrolment cells: JK, K, grades 1, 2, 3 and 4–8. Sum those cells for class enrolment. Average = total enrolment across the selected rows ÷ number of non-empty class rows. A board’s mean is calculated from classes, not by averaging school means.

Grade groups include classes exclusively within that band. A grades 2/3 split is “Grades 1–3”; a grades 3/4 split is “Across grade bands”. The latter combines every cross-band configuration, including kindergarten/primary. “All” pools the source’s non-empty class rows.

For each suppressed cell, add 1 to the lower total and 4 to the upper total. Divide both totals by the same class count. For the share above 25, the lower count includes classes certainly above 25; the upper count also includes classes that might exceed 25. Zero-enrolment rows are excluded and counted in the coverage export. These are mathematical bounds on suppressed counts, not sampling uncertainty.

School lines break at missing or ambiguous years. Pandemic remote-learning arrangements, school changes and the kindergarten expansion can affect comparisons. No trend is presented as a causal effect of funding.
