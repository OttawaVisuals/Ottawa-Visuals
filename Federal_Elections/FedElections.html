The April 28, 2025 general election riding by riding: the actual result, the seats won with a true majority, and a model where the votes of several parties are combined behind whichever of them led in each riding. Choose the grouping (for example Liberal + NDP) and how many of the dropped candidates' voters follow. It is arithmetic about vote-splitting under a simple transfer assumption, not a forecast, a ranked-ballot count or a voting recommendation. Click a riding for its own numbers.

How the model works
?
In every riding, whichever of the selected parties actually got the most votes becomes the single "survivor"; the others step aside. The slider controls how many of their voters would actually follow and vote for the survivor instead of staying home or voting elsewhere. 100% assumes perfect transfer, which real-world consolidation never achieves. Moving it switches the map to "Modelled results" and drives the modelled gauge, sensitive-seat list and table below. Every party outside the selected group, including the Bloc Québécois, keeps its votes and can still win.

Methodology

Sources and scope

April 28, 2025 results: Elections Canada's 45th general election official voting results, Table 12 (all 1,959 candidates) and Table 11 (343 districts). Each riding's candidate votes are checked to sum to its valid ballots, and the elected candidate in Table 11 must be the top vote-getter. Actual seats reconcile to the official result: Liberal 169, Conservative 144, Bloc Québécois 22, NDP 7, Green 1. By-elections and changes in affiliation since the election are excluded.

Federal electoral district boundaries (2023 representation order) are drawn from Elections Canada's shapefile in its original Lambert conformal conic projection, joined to results by district number. The national map is simplified by 2.5 km with tiny islands omitted; the Ottawa, Toronto, Montréal and Vancouver insets use 60 m simplification and show the ridings whose centres lie within a fixed distance of a central riding. Turnout for 2015, 2019 and 2021 is the sum of Elections Canada's provincial totals (Table 4); those elections used the earlier 338-seat boundaries, so only national turnout is compared.

Consolidation assumption

In each riding the leading candidate among the selected parties keeps their actual votes. The slider adds the chosen share of the other selected parties' votes to that candidate. Every party outside the group, including the Bloc Québécois, independents and all minor parties, keeps its actual votes and can still win; this is an explicit scenario choice, not evidence about their voters' preferences.

65% is an illustrative starting setting, with no federal calibration. Votes from the dropped candidates that do not transfer are left unallocated; they are not assigned to anyone. No new voters, campaign effects, candidate effects or turnout responses are modelled. Actual vote-share denominators and turnout remain the recorded values. It is a one-step transfer, not a ranked-ballot or instant-runoff count, a forecast or a voting recommendation.

Winners, majorities and ties

Actual winners have the most valid candidate votes. A majority requires more than 50% of valid votes, excluding rejected ballots. Minor parties are combined in the table's "Others" column for display only; they compete as individual candidates when determining the modelled winner. A modelled tie has no assigned winner and is counted separately from flips.

The transfer boundary is (winner's votes − group leader's votes) ÷ votes of the other selected parties. The group leader must strictly exceed that boundary to win. The ranked cards show the smallest whole-percent slider setting that wins; boundaries at or above 100% are not reachable. Thresholds apply only to ridings won by a party outside the selected group.

Survey evidence

The survey section uses the campaign-period wave of the 2025 Canadian Election Study (Stephenson, Harell and Rubenson; Harvard Dataverse; licence CC BY-NC 4.0), 11,577 weighted respondents who named a party they would vote for. Weights are the study's high-quality-respondent weights, raked to the 2021 census by province, gender, age and education; the Territories have no weights and are excluded. "Open to" means the respondent did not say they would absolutely not vote for that party and rated it 50 or higher on a 0–100 slider. These are stated attitudes at one point in the campaign, not observed behaviour, and the study is an online panel, so treat the figures as a rough guide to the 0–100% slider range, not as a calibrated transfer rate. Postal code is not public, so regions are provinces or groups of provinces; no riding or Ottawa figure is available. Groups under 100 respondents are not shown. The extra tables use the post-election wave (same respondents) for actual vote, reasons for choosing a party and reasons for not voting, with its own high-quality weight. "Didn't want another party to win" is one response option to the question about the most important reason for choosing a party, so it is a stated-motive measure of tactical voting, not an observed one. Issue answers are free text sorted by keyword. Only aggregated tables are stored here (ces_tables.json); the microdata is not redistributed.
