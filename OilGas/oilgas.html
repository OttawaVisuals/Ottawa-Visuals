Canada · oil & gas industry · 2002–2025

Oil & gas: record profits, fewer jobs

How much Canada's oil and gas producers earned, what they paid out to shareholders and in corporate income tax, and how many people they employ. All from Statistics Canada, and all dollar figures in dollars (adjusted for inflation).

Profits

Boom, bust, and the biggest boom yet

Net income after tax, industry "Oil and gas extraction and support services" (StatCan 33-10-0500-01, annual, to 2024; summed from the quarterly table 33-10-0225-01, which comes within about 5% of the annual figures in the years both cover). The big losses are mostly write-downs: companies cutting the book value of oil sands and other assets when prices crashed. These are accounting charges, not cash leaving the business. Dividends are what companies declared to shareholders in the year.

Jobs by province,

Employees on payroll (StatCan SEPH, 14-10-0202-01), not counting self-employed contractors. "Support activities" is NAICS 213: drilling contractors, well services and similar. StatCan doesn't split it between oil & gas and mining. In Alberta it is almost all oil & gas; nationally a minority is mining support, so the national total is a slight overcount. Pipelines are excluded: StatCan suppresses that series after 2002.

Corporate income tax

What the industry paid in corporate income tax

This is not the whole government take. Royalties paid to provinces for the oil and gas itself are a much bigger flow. StatCan counts them as a business expense, so they are already subtracted before profit and don't appear here. Royalties and government support (subsidies, Trans Mountain, loan guarantees) are the next section to build.

Federal + provincial corporate income tax from StatCan 33-10-0500-01 (taxation statistics, latest year 2024). Tax is assessed on taxable income, which differs from book profit: losses from 2015–2020 are carried forward and reduce tax in later years, and resource deductions differ between tax and accounting rules.

Coming next

Government money in and out

There is no official yearly series for government support to oil and gas. The next version will add it as labelled estimates, each bar tied to its source, rather than one stitched-together line:

  • Money in to governments: provincial royalties (Alberta, Saskatchewan, BC, Newfoundland budgets).
  • Money out, federal: Trans Mountain, carbon-capture tax credits, Export Development Canada financing, the 2020 well-cleanup program.
  • Money out, Alberta: Keystone XL, the Sturgeon refinery, orphan-well funding, royalty credits.
  • Independent tallies (Environmental Defence, IISD) and the Auditor General's 2023 review, with how each defines "subsidy".
