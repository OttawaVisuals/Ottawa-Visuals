Vote coordination assumption

?
In every riding, whichever of LIB / NDP / Green actually got the most votes becomes the single "unity" candidate; the other two step aside. The slider controls how many of their voters would actually follow and vote for the survivor instead of staying home or voting elsewhere — 100% assumes perfect transfer, which real-world vote consolidation never achieves. This feeds the "Modelled results" option on the map and gauges below.

65% is a reasonable starting point.

?
On the base number: studies of strategic/anti-splitting voting in similar first-past-the-post systems generally find 50–75% of a dropped candidate's voters will switch to block a disliked front-runner, with the rest staying home or voting sincerely anyway. A full merger of party machines (not just a single-riding stand-down) can push retention toward 80–90%, but that's a different, bigger ask than what this slider models. Move it to see how sensitive the result is.

Methodology

Sources

Riding-level vote counts and province-wide turnout/seat history come from Elections Ontario's public Voting Results Explorer exports. Electoral district boundaries come from Elections Ontario's ELECTORAL_DISTRICT shapefile, cut in mapshaper into Ontario.json, Ottawa.json, and SouthernOntario.json — the latter two are higher-resolution cuts of the same districts, used only for the Ottawa and Southern Ontario zoom insets.

Map projection

District shapes are plotted directly in the shapefile's own projected coordinate system (Ontario Lambert Conformal Conic, in metres), not reprojected to latitude/longitude. This keeps the hand-cut rectangular district shapes exactly as drawn; converting to lat/lon would add a few degrees of grid-convergence rotation and skew them.

Results joining

Each riding's results are joined to its boundary by name, normalized for case, accents, and dash style. "Winner" is whichever party received the most votes in a riding (plurality), not necessarily a majority.

Majority results

A seat counts as a "true majority" only if the winning party took more than 50% of the votes cast in that riding. Seats won with a plurality under 50% are shown grey on the map and in the gauge.

Modelled results

This is a simple hypothetical reallocation, not a forecast. In every riding, whichever of LIB / NDP / Green actually received the most votes becomes a single "survivor" candidate; the votes of the other two are multiplied by the slider's retention percentage and added to the survivor's own vote total. Every other party's votes are left unchanged. The riding's hypothetical winner is then whichever of PCP / the survivor / all other parties combined ends up with the most votes under that reallocation. It assumes turnout and every other vote stays fixed — a real merger or coordinated campaign would change turnout and candidate behaviour in ways this model doesn't capture.

Regenerating the data

All of data.js, geo.js, and turnout.js are generated from the raw Elections Ontario exports by build_data.py; re-run it after dropping in new export files and reload this page.
