Ontario Elections Visual — Methodology
=======================================
(Mirrors the "Methodology" section on ONelections.html. This plain-text copy
exists so the method is readable without opening the page, and so it survives
independently of any future redesign of that section.)

SOURCES
-------
Riding-level vote counts and province-wide turnout/seat history come from
Elections Ontario's public Voting Results Explorer exports:
  - Explorer_All_*.csv            candidate-level votes by riding, 2025 general election
  - Explorer_Election_*.csv       province-wide registered/turnout counts, 1981-2025
  - Explorer_Party_*.csv          province-wide seats won by party, per election year
  - Explorer_Electoral_District_*.csv   riding-level registered/turnout counts, 2018/2022/2025

Electoral district boundaries come from Elections Ontario's ELECTORAL_DISTRICT
shapefile, cut in mapshaper into three GeoJSON files:
  - Ontario.json          all 124 ridings, province-wide map
  - Ottawa.json            higher-resolution cut of the Ottawa-area ridings
  - SouthernOntario.json   higher-resolution cut of the Southern Ontario ridings
Ottawa.json and SouthernOntario.json are not separate datasets — they are the
same ridings as Ontario.json, re-cut at higher resolution purely so the two
zoom insets on the map look crisp at their larger on-screen size.

MAP PROJECTION
--------------
District shapes are plotted directly in the shapefile's own projected
coordinate system (Ontario Lambert Conformal Conic, in metres — see
ELECTORAL_DISTRICT.shp.xml / the .prj), not reprojected to latitude/longitude.
This keeps the hand-cut rectangular district shapes exactly as drawn;
converting to lat/lon would add a few degrees of grid-convergence rotation and
skew them. Practically: build_data.py wraps the three GeoJSON files into
geo.js untouched (no merging, no coordinate transform), and ONelections.html
renders their coordinates as a literal Cartesian plane.

RESULTS JOINING
----------------
Each riding's results are joined to its boundary by name, normalized for
case, accents, and dash style (see the `norm()` function in ONelections.html).
As of the 2026-06-22 build, this join is 100% clean: every riding in the vote
data matches a boundary in Ontario.json and vice versa, and every riding has
turnout history for 2018/2022/2025 — re-run build_data.py after any data
refresh and check its printed diagnostics before trusting a rebuild.

"Winner" is whichever party received the most votes in a riding (plurality),
not necessarily a majority.

MAJORITY RESULTS
-----------------
A seat counts as a "true majority" only if the winning party took more than
50% of the votes cast in that riding. Seats won with a plurality under 50%
are shown grey on the map and in the gauge ("no majority").

MODELLED RESULTS (vote-coordination scenario)
------------------------------------------------
This is a simple hypothetical reallocation, not a forecast.

In every riding, whichever of Liberal / NDP / Green actually received the
most votes becomes a single "survivor" candidate; the other two are treated
as having stood aside. The votes of those two dropped candidates are
multiplied by the slider's retention percentage (0-100%, default 65%) and
added to the survivor's own vote total — this is the "coordinated votes"
figure. Every other party's votes (PCP, and everything outside the four core
parties, bucketed as "Others") are left unchanged.

The riding's hypothetical winner is then whichever of PCP / the survivor /
Others ends up with the most votes under that reallocation.

Assumptions this deliberately does NOT try to model, because a real merger or
coordinated campaign would change these in ways a static reallocation cannot
capture:
  - Turnout is held fixed. In reality, a unity candidate could draw out
    voters who stayed home, or discourage others.
  - No candidate-quality or campaign effects. The model assumes 100% of
    "transferred" votes land on the survivor regardless of who that
    candidate actually is.
  - PCP's own vote total is held fixed. A different opposition landscape
    could change PCP's turnout or vote share too.

On the 65% default: studies of strategic/anti-splitting voting in similar
first-past-the-post systems generally find 50-75% of a dropped candidate's
voters will switch to block a disliked front-runner, with the rest staying
home or voting sincerely anyway. A full merger of party machines (not just a
single-riding stand-down) can push retention toward 80-90%, but that is a
different, bigger ask than a single-riding stand-down.

STRATEGIC-CANDIDATE ANALYSIS (flip threshold)
------------------------------------------------
For every PCP-held riding, the "flip threshold" is the minimum share of the
dropped candidates' votes the survivor would need to overtake PCP's ACTUAL
vote count — solved directly, independent of where the slider happens to be
set:

    pctNeeded = (pcpVotes - survivorVotes) / droppedVotes

Three special cases:
  - "already ahead" — the survivor already out-polls PCP with zero vote
    transfer under this model (PCP still won the seat on the real, split
    vote — this is about the model's counterfactual, not the actual result).
  - "not reachable" — there are no dropped opposition votes to transfer in
    that riding (droppedVotes = 0), so no amount of coordination changes the
    outcome under this model.
  - Otherwise, a percentage. Values over 100% are shown as "100%+" — not
    achievable even at full retention.

The "Most winnable PCP-held seats" list on the page ranks every reachable
riding (0-100% threshold) by this number, lowest first: the ridings where
opposition coordination would go furthest for the least vote-transfer
assumption. The same number appears in a "Strategic voting" callout when a
PCP-held riding is selected on the map.

Limit worth knowing: this only asks whether the survivor's coordinated total
would exceed PCP's — it does not check whether "Others" might still end up
ahead of both instead. That is a rare edge case in this dataset but not
impossible in principle (e.g. a strong independent or minor-party riding).

REGENERATING THE DATA
------------------------
All of data.js, geo.js, and turnout.js are generated from the raw Elections
Ontario exports by build_data.py:

    python Ontario_Elections/build_data.py

Re-run it after dropping in new export files, then reload the page. The
script prints join-coverage diagnostics (ridings in the vote data missing
from the boundary file and vice versa, districts missing turnout history) —
read those before trusting a rebuild; a name-matching regression would show
up there before it shows up as a silently blank district on the map.
