What these numbers mean

Counts and percentages

Criminal: a case means the charges on one Information for one accused. Cases are classified by the most serious charge. One person can have several cases; these are not counts of unique people. The all-criminal series includes youth cases.

Provincial offences: individual charges under Parts I and III of the Provincial Offences Act, including Highway Traffic Act offences and other statutes. These are charges, not criminal cases or unique people. Parking matters under Part II are outside this series.

Share: category count ÷ the published completed total for the same subject and period. A zero completed total has no percentage. Received and pending counts are context only; neither is used as the denominator. No totals are added across reporting periods.

How outcomes are grouped

Guilty pleas before trial and at a trial date are combined. For criminal cases, “withdrawn/stayed before trial” remains separate from withdrawals at a trial date. Stays cannot be separated from withdrawals in the pre-trial category. Other dispositions before and at trial dates are combined.

For provincial offences, prepaid fines, guilty pleas, guilt recorded after failure to respond/attend, withdrawals, other dispositions and completion following trial remain separate categories. “Following a trial” is a process category, not a guilty or acquitted result.

“At trial date” can mean the matter was resolved without a trial. The page does not label guilty pleas as trial verdicts. No complete conviction/acquittal rate, reason for withdrawal or stay, or finding about police conduct can be calculated from these tables.

Coverage, periods and source limitations

The calendar-year view excludes publications with different reporting windows. The criminal file indexed as 2020 covers October 2019–September 2020; the files indexed as 2026 cover April 2025–March 2026. These are complete reporting windows, not 2026 year-to-date. Full dates control the labels, rather than filenames.

The Court cautions about the accuracy and reliability of pandemic-era ICON data. Changes in legislation, enforcement, offence mix and court operations can affect comparisons. Provincial traffic counts can include automated-camera charges; no adjustment for enforcement volume is made.

These are Ottawa OCJ location statistics and Ottawa City provincial-offence statistics. They are not all courts in Ottawa, do not identify individual cases or judges, and cannot be linked to named OPS officers. Superior Court case outcomes are not included.

The Ministry of the Attorney General collects the data on behalf of the Court. The Court does not confirm its accuracy/completeness or endorse this analysis. Newer source workbooks include reuse/audience wording; this report retains the original links and source notices in its audit.

Extraction and reconciliation

Discover annual publications from the official statistics index. Read only the Ottawa or Ottawa City workbook sheet, or the Ottawa PDF table. Keep the original period, table row, source URL and file hash with every extracted row. Source workbooks are read without changing formulas or labels.

Check that the component outcomes sum to each published row’s before-trial, trial-date and completed totals. Separately compare offence rows against group totals and groups/statutes against the overall total. Source inconsistencies remain recorded; no missing count is distributed across categories. The page uses the source’s printed row for each selected group or overall total.

Blank count cells are treated as zero within structurally valid rows only after their outcome totals reconcile. Missing offence rows, source periods and failed downloads are never turned into zero. Header errors in some workbooks are ignored only outside the numeric data table.
