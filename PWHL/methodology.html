Data & methodology

Source: the PWHL's own stats feed (HockeyTech), fetched by a daily-scheduled pipeline in PWHL/scripts/daily_update.R and pre-aggregated into the JSON files this page loads by PWHL/scripts/build_dashboard_json.py.

  • Covers three seasons: 2024 (inaugural), 2024-25, and 2025-26, plus each season's playoffs.
  • "Player leaders" uses regular-season totals only; playoff stats aren't blended in.
  • Team colours are each club's dominant brand colour, approximated by eye (the league publishes colour names, not codes); the darkest ones are lightened a little on the dark theme so they stay visible.
  • Attendance: a game counts as Takeover Tour / neutral site when its venue is outside the home team's own market (e.g. Ottawa's games in Calgary or Winnipeg). Big-arena games inside the market (Canadian Tire Centre, Bell Centre, Scotiabank Arena) count as home-market games. Capacity is the hockey capacity from PWHL/data/PWHL_Venues.csv, so a sold-out game can read slightly above or below 100%; games at venues with no listed capacity are left out of the % figures.
  • "Superlatives" uses skater totals for the selected season (regular, preseason, or playoff, matching the season picked). Players traded mid-season appear once, with their split rows summed (marked *); the feed also emits occasional exact-duplicate stat lines, which are dropped.
  • Per-60 rankings divide the stat by ice time (stat × 3600 / seconds on ice). Eligibility minimums scale with the season's length — roughly 8 games and 120 minutes over a full regular season, proportionally lower for a short playoff run — so a three-game call-up with one lucky shift doesn't win everything.
  • Goalies are absent from the roster feed's position data and from these skater stat lines, so every superlative is a skater award.
  • No "hit the post/crossbar" award: the play-by-play feed records shots on goal, blocked shots, and goals, but never missed shots, so iron is invisible in this data.
  • The shot map, goals-by-period, and event-mix charts are drawn from the full play-by-play record across all seasons combined, not filtered to the season selector above — a per-season breakdown is a natural next step.
  • Coordinates in the play-by-play feed are in feet from center ice/center width, matching a standard 200×85 ft rink; they aren't normalized for which end a team was attacking, so the map shows raw shot locations rather than "offensive zone" shots.
  • Forecast (PWHL/scripts/build_elo.py): Elo ratings updated after every regular-season and playoff game (preseason ignored). Settings are chosen by testing them on past seasons. The test scores are nearly identical across a wide range of settings, so among those within 0.001 log loss of the best, the most responsive is used. Playoff odds come from simulating the rest of the schedule and the playoff bracket 10,000 times, with ratings updating inside each simulation. The 2026-27 format (two six-team conferences, top four qualify, best-of-3 then best-of-5 rounds) is from the league's 2 Oct 2026 schedule announcement. Playoff games already played aren't fed into the bracket simulation yet.
  • Expected goals (xG) come from a logistic regression fitted in PWHL/scripts/build_models.py on every shot on goal with a goalie in net. Inputs are distance and angle to the net, shot type, rebound (team shot in the previous 3 s), rush (previous event outside the offensive zone within 4 s) and manpower. The feed doesn't say which end a team attacked, so that's inferred per period from where its shots cluster. Manpower is rebuilt from the penalty log and matches the feed's own power-play flag on about 99% of goals.
  • The feed records shots on goal but not missed shots, so this xG only covers shots that reached the goalie. That lowers its accuracy compared with NHL models that also see misses. Each league year is re-centred so total xG equals actual goals, which keeps one season's goalies from all looking good or bad together. The calibration chart shows the remaining error, which is largest on the highest-danger shots.
  • Player impact ratings are in goals above an average player at the same position, for the selected season. Two versions exist:
    • On-ice lists at goals: on a goal (both its shot row and its goal row) the feed often lists the skaters from both sides of the next line change, 7-10 names a team. Those lists are rebuilt from the goal's plus/minus players, which are exactly who was on. Before this fix (10 Oct 2026) the 5-on-5 ratings silently skipped about half of all even-strength goals; correcting it moved the median player's rating by about 0.1 goals.
    • Full model (any season where the play-by-play lists who was on the ice, which today is every season): even-strength offence and defence come from a ridge regression of 5-on-5 xG rates on every skater on the ice at once, which separates a player from their linemates and opponents. Ice time between events is approximated from those on-ice lists.
    • Box-score estimate (fallback for a season without on-ice data): shot generation (individual xG above position average for the minutes played) and playmaking (0.5 × primary + 0.25 × secondary assists above average) stand in for it. These totals are larger and noisier and shouldn't be compared directly with full-model seasons. The note under the ratings says which version a season uses.
    Both versions add finishing (goals minus xG, shrunk toward zero by shots ÷ (shots + 300) because shooting luck is large in a 30-game season), penalties (minors taken versus position average × that season's power-play goals per minor) and faceoffs (0.01 goals per win above 50%). Goalies are rated by GSAx. Weights marked as assumptions here are set by hand, not fitted.
  • Team pages: the roster and stats follow the season picked; the "most used lines" are 5-on-5 forward trios and defence pairs ranked by time on ice together, rebuilt from the on-ice lists in the play-by-play (same approximation as the ratings, so treat ice time as close, not exact). Franchise leaders add up each player's impact rating over every regular season with the club.
  • Player pages: shot mix and goalie matchups come from the expected-goals model, goalie-in-net shots only. About a third of shots have no type in the feed ("not recorded"). The role tag ranks the player's impact rating against everyone at the same position that season.
  • The Shin-Pad Shooter counts a shot attempt as blocked when the feed logs a blocked shot with that player as the shooter. Missed shots aren't in the feed, so attempts = shots on goal + blocked. The Rap Sheet counts penalty events by infraction. The Hall of Sustained Quirk uses the totals view, regular seasons only, and needs an outright (untied) win.
  • This is a first draft built to show the range of what the data supports — most sections are candidates for refinement, not final designs.
