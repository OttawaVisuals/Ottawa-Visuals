Data & methodology

Source: the PWHL's own stats feed (HockeyTech), fetched by a daily-scheduled pipeline in PWHL/scripts/daily_update.R and pre-aggregated into the JSON files this page loads by PWHL/scripts/build_dashboard_json.py.

  • Covers three seasons: 2024 (inaugural), 2024-25, and 2025-26, plus each season's playoffs.
  • "Player leaders" uses regular-season totals only; playoff stats aren't blended in.
  • "Superlatives" uses skater totals for the selected season (regular, preseason, or playoff, matching the season picked). Players traded mid-season appear once, with their split rows summed (marked *); the feed also emits occasional exact-duplicate stat lines, which are dropped.
  • Per-60 rankings divide the stat by ice time (stat × 3600 / seconds on ice). Eligibility minimums scale with the season's length — roughly 8 games and 120 minutes over a full regular season, proportionally lower for a short playoff run — so a three-game call-up with one lucky shift doesn't win everything.
  • Goalies are absent from the roster feed's position data and from these skater stat lines, so every superlative is a skater award.
  • No "hit the post/crossbar" award: the play-by-play feed records shots on goal, blocked shots, and goals, but never missed shots, so iron is invisible in this data.
  • The shot map, goals-by-period, and event-mix charts are drawn from the full play-by-play record across all seasons combined, not filtered to the season selector above — a per-season breakdown is a natural next step.
  • Coordinates in the play-by-play feed are in feet from center ice/center width, matching a standard 200×85 ft rink; they aren't normalized for which end a team was attacking, so the map shows raw shot locations rather than "offensive zone" shots.
  • Expected goals (xG) come from a logistic regression fitted in PWHL/scripts/build_models.py on every shot on goal with a goalie in net. Inputs are distance and angle to the net, shot type, rebound (team shot in the previous 3 s), rush (previous event outside the offensive zone within 4 s) and manpower. The feed doesn't say which end a team attacked, so that's inferred per period from where its shots cluster. Manpower is rebuilt from the penalty log and matches the feed's own power-play flag on about 99% of goals.
  • The feed records shots on goal but not missed shots, so this xG only covers shots that reached the goalie. That lowers its accuracy compared with NHL models that also see misses. Each league year is re-centred so total xG equals actual goals, which keeps one season's goalies from all looking good or bad together. The calibration chart shows the remaining error, which is largest on the highest-danger shots.
  • Player impact ratings are in goals above an average player at the same position, for the selected season. Two versions exist:
    • Full model (2025-26 onward, when the feed started listing who was on the ice): even-strength offence and defence come from a ridge regression of 5-on-5 xG rates on every skater on the ice at once, which separates a player from their linemates and opponents. Ice time between events is approximated from those on-ice lists.
    • Box-score estimate (earlier seasons): no on-ice data, so shot generation (individual xG above position average for the minutes played) and playmaking (0.5 × primary + 0.25 × secondary assists above average) stand in for it. These totals are larger and noisier and shouldn't be compared directly with full-model seasons.
    Both versions add finishing (goals minus xG, shrunk toward zero by shots ÷ (shots + 300) because shooting luck is large in a 30-game season), penalties (minors taken versus position average × that season's power-play goals per minor) and faceoffs (0.01 goals per win above 50%). Goalies are rated by GSAx. Weights marked as assumptions here are set by hand, not fitted.
  • This is a first draft built to show the range of what the data supports — most sections are candidates for refinement, not final designs.
