# Sutcliffe’s original 2022 promises

Topics: municipal property-tax increases (2013–2026), adult transit fares (late-2022 baseline through 2026), official bus reliability KPIs, housing delivery and municipal savings. The tax promise comparison applies only to 2023–2026; the fare-freeze commitment applies only to 2023. Reviewed October 24, 2026. No 2026 campaign commitments included.

## Data and methodology

`data/property-tax.json` is the manually reviewed source of truth: annual increases, commitment type, source URLs, document locations and evidence limitations. `scripts/build_property_tax.py` generates the standalone `promises.html` and downloadable CSV using Python’s standard library. No browser fetch or chart dependency; content remains readable offline and without JavaScript.

Rebuild: `python Promises/scripts/build_property_tax.py`. Validate: `python Promises/scripts/check_property_tax.py` and `node --check Promises/promises.js`.

The chart uses vertical SVG bars (years on x, increases on y), horizontal 2% and 2.5% references within 2023–2026, and an optional CPI line on the same percentage axis. Controls switch between all years and Sutcliffe’s four budgets; `range=term` and `inflation=0` are shareable URL parameters. All years remain in the exact table/CSV. Native SVG titles give point values; the table provides touch/keyboard access. Narrow screens scroll horizontally.

Annual/Cumulative controls use `measure=cumulative` in the URL. Cumulative taxes compound each annual budget rate: `(product(1 + rate/100) − 1) × 100`, starting from 2012 for the full history or 2022 for Sutcliffe’s budgets. This is an illustrative tax index, not a household bill or total levy revenue. Cumulative CPI uses `(endpoint index / baseline annual index − 1) × 100`. The 2026 endpoint is the Jan–Sep CPI average; earlier endpoints are annual averages. Do not compound 2026’s matched-period YTD rate onto the full 2025 annual index. The graph labels this difference in endpoint coverage. Annual promise references are hidden in cumulative mode. `data/property-tax-cumulative.csv` and a second exact table retain both baseline series; the original annual CSV is unchanged.

Compare the adopted overall municipal increase with the 2.5% upper benchmark. The original framework limits 2023–24 to 2–2.5% and targets the same in 2025–26. Do not turn the latter into an unconditional four-year cap. Values are 2.5%, 2.5%, 3.9%, 3.75%; gaps are 0, 0, 1.4, 1.25 percentage points.

The campaign source is contemporary CityNews coverage; the original archived platform has not been obtained. Its opening paragraph has a typo, but headline and detailed framework agree. The City’s own historical budget comparison table supplies 2013–2023: 2.09%, 1.91%, 2% for 2015–2018, 3% for 2019–2022 and 2.5% for 2023. Earlier years receive no promise assessment. 2024–25 use council members’ contemporary adoption accounts; 2026 uses the City adoption announcement. Source notes remain visible on the page.

`data/inflation.json` retains Statistics Canada annual CPI levels for 2012–2025, January–September monthly observations for 2025/2026, calculated changes and API response hashes. Geography: Ottawa-Gatineau, Ontario part; all-items, not seasonally adjusted. Annual formula: `(CPI(year) / CPI(year−1) − 1) × 100`. 2026 uses `(mean(Jan–Sep 2026) / mean(Jan–Sep 2025) − 1) × 100`, yielding approximately 2.6%. Do not compare a partial-year mean against the previous full-year mean. The dashed segment and hollow point flag the partial year. CPI is a consumer-price benchmark, not a municipal cost index; same-year inflation was not fully known at budget adoption.

To deliberately refresh CPI: `python Promises/scripts/fetch_cpi.py`, review the cutoff and inputs, then rebuild and validate. The fetch script stops if the 2026 published cutoff differs from September. This is separate from Budget’s inflation factors and does not modify that page’s inputs.

The metric is not total levy revenue growth, an individual bill, a statutory tax-rate series or an inflation-adjusted amount. Distinguish transit’s levy from transit fares. Excludes separate fees, provincial education taxes and assessment changes. Avoid cumulative household estimates from these rounded overall figures.

## Housing

`data/housing.json` retains 21 annual/YTD completion observations (2006–2026), 80 monthly completion observations (Jan 2020–Aug 2026), construction stages for 2023–2026 and three years of affordable/supportive report results. `scripts/build_housing.py` generates four static SVGs, exact tables, `housing.csv` (121 observations; annual/monthly/subset records must not be added together), and `housing-progress.json`. No PDF or spreadsheet runtime dependency for ordinary page builds.

The original October 14, 2022 Glebe Report campaign advertisement (PDF p. 34) states 100,000 homes over ten years, including 10,000 affordable homes. September 16 CityNews platform coverage instead uses community housing and specifies 1,000 per year. Neither reviewed source fixes the exact calendar start. This page uses January 2023, the first full year after taking office, as an explicit analytical convention; late 2022 is excluded. The 10,000/year straight-line pace is arithmetic, not a promised interim milestone. In the cumulative view its 2026 endpoint is prorated to eight months. The full-year reference stops before the partial 2026 annual bar. No final target deadline or definitive campaign completion percentage is assigned. The provincial 151,000-home pledge is a different commitment and is not substituted.

City HNA Table 31 (PDF p. 71) supplies history through 2023; all components reconcile to totals. Its prose mistakenly repeats 7,668 as 2023; the table’s 7,890 is used. Recent construction data is the public councillor-office compilation of CMHC census-subdivision releases, linked by the Kitchissippi Ward site. The workbook’s Notes tab identifies the CSD source. This is a secondary compilation, not an independent current CMHC pull (automated access blocked). Monthly/YTD sums, dwelling-type components and the overlap with the HNA (2020–2023) reconcile exactly. 2024 completions: 8,794; 2025: 7,797; Jan–Aug 2026: 5,559. Cumulative Jan 2023–Aug 2026: 30,040. Jan–Aug 2025: 4,514 is the matched-period comparison. Geography is Ottawa city/CSD, excluding neighbouring municipalities and Gatineau. No net-stock or causal claim.

City program reports give 49 affordable + 57 supportive completions in 2023, 318 + 75 in 2024 and 31 + 48 in 2025: combined 578. The categories are not proven identical to campaign community/affordable scope; do not interpret this as exact fulfilment of the 10,000-unit pledge. The 2023 report separately discusses a late-2022 completed project with 2023 move-ins; these 40 units are not added again. Under-construction figures are stocks, kept separate from completions. No 2026 program delivery count is inferred. People housed, benefits and acquisitions are not counted as new-unit completions.

Context uses year-end annual reports: 2024 wait list 14,721 and placements 1,077. The HNA’s Dec 10 wait-list snapshot is 15,140 and its placement figure 1,089; discrepancies are preserved without assuming the reason. Budget’s existing series is unchanged. HNA starts-table figures conflict with its narrative; starts here use only the councillor’s municipality compilation.

`scripts/extract_housing_sources.py` deliberately refreshes the reviewed snapshot using cached PDF/workbook sources in the OS temporary directory. Requires pymupdf and openpyxl; captures source SHA-256 hashes and cell/page locations. Blank future component cells with formula totals of zero are excluded as unreported, not recorded zero months. The script stops if the August 2026 cutoff changes; review labels before refreshing. 2025 program figures are manually reviewed against the official June 26, 2026 memo’s indexed text (direct opening returned 403). Primary PDF pages and the campaign ad were visually reviewed after rendering.

Housing controls use `housingview=cumulative` and `housinghistory=1`, scoped independently from other topics. Validate with `python Promises/scripts/check_housing.py` and `node --check Promises/housing.js`.

## Bus reliability

`scripts/build_bus_reliability.py` reuses `OC_Transpo/kpi_history/kpi_series.csv`, filtering official bus delivery, punctuality and frequent-route regularity, without breakdowns. Retains annual results and monthly observations from 2022 onward: 63 observations total. No collector rebuild or edits. The complete page builder invokes it. `data/bus-reliability.json` retains input SHA-256, original row metadata, source URLs, three May 2026 targets and publication/extraction details. `bus-source-links.json` stores five public document/spreadsheet mappings derived from the existing scraper manifest; rebuilds do not require ignored local PDF downloads or manifest files. New source files must be mapped explicitly.

Annual figures: delivery 97.8/98/97% for 2023/24/25; punctuality 74.5/74/72%; regularity 82/82% for 2024/25 (2023 unavailable). Annual narrative assertions stop the build if the referenced observations change, requiring review. No complete annual 2022 or 2026 result is inferred. Monthly delivery includes Aug 2022–Aug 2023 and Apr 2025–Apr 2026; punctuality Jan 2025–Apr 2026; regularity Apr 2025–Apr 2026. Earlier pandemic-era punctuality remains on the full KPI dashboard, not used as a clean baseline here. Missing months are not joined or interpolated.

Annual bars start at zero; monthly lines use labelled narrowed axes (90–100 delivery, 60–100 punctuality, 70–100 regularity). Reference targets come from the May 2026 scorecard: 99.5%, 85%, 85%. Delivery target completion is Q4 2027; regularity’s date depends on reaching service availability; no extracted punctuality deadline is asserted. These are not numerical promises made in 2022 or presumed historical deadlines. No whole-promise pass/fail verdict or causal attribution.

`busmetric` and `busperiod=monthly` URL parameters preserve selection; controls are independent from tax/fare views. Exports include exact observations and source links. Spreadsheet extracts carry a legacy .pdf source filename in the archive; this page links the actual .xlsx and identifies the worksheet. Live predictions and early/late splits are excluded because the comparable official series does not provide them consistently. Validation: `python Promises/scripts/check_bus_reliability.py` and `node --check Promises/bus-reliability.js`.

## Transit fares

`data/transit-fares.json` retains reviewed adult card single-ride, cash single-ride and monthly-pass prices. Late-2022 baseline: $3.70 / $3.75 / $125.50, unchanged in 2023; 2024: $3.80 / $3.85 / $128.75; 2025: $4.00 / $4.05 / $135.00; 2026: $4.10 / $4.15 / $138.50. The 2022 baseline is a schedule in effect before this term, not a full-year average.

`scripts/build_transit_fares.py` builds static SVG charts and exact exports; the main `build_property_tax.py` invokes it when assembling the complete page. `transit-fares-series.json` retains derived price changes and CPI comparisons. Annual change = `(current / previous − 1) × 100`; cumulative = `(current / late-2022 baseline − 1) × 100`. Actual price rounding matters: card fares rose 2.70% in 2024 despite the 2.5% budget headline. No 2022 increase is inferred. CPI follows the same annual/matched-YTD and cumulative endpoint methods as property taxes.

Price/Annual increase/Cumulative modes and a product selector use shareable URL fields `fare=card|cash|monthly`, `fareview=price|annual|cumulative`, `farecpi=0` to hide inflation. Tax and fare controls are scoped independently and preserve each other’s URL parameters. Price charts use CAD; CPI is available only on the percentage axis. Without JavaScript, the adult card price chart and all tables/sources/downloads remain accessible; local file controls use embedded series data without fetching.

Sources include the 2026 adopted City budget’s 2025/2026 user-fee table, implemented 2024 before/after reporting, operator announcements republished by councillors and the operator’s 2023 freeze statement. Operator pages returned 403 on direct automated opening; indexed official text was available. Source notes distinguish proposal, implementation and cross-check evidence. Standard adult products only; seniors/youth/discounted/Para Transpo fares require a separate review. Fare caps, transfers, payment availability, free-service periods and actual rider spending are not modelled.

Validate: `python Promises/scripts/check_transit_fares.py` and `node --check Promises/transit-fares.js`.
