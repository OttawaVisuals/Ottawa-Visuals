01 · Affordability

Property-tax increases

The 2022 commitment

Limit increases to 2–2.5% in 2023 and 2024; target the same range in 2025 and 2026.

Paraphrase of the October 5, 2022 financial framework. Read the campaign coverage ↗

The 2023–24 commitment was met. Both years came in at 2.5%. Increases of 3.9% in 2025 and 3.75% in 2026 exceeded the later-year target.

These are the published overall municipal increases in Council-approved budgets. The comparison preserves the distinction between a firm commitment and a later target.

Why 3.9% in 2025? The citywide and police levies rose 2.9%, while the transit levy rose 8%. Their weighted overall impact was published as 3.9%. Transit fare increases are a separate charge and are not added to this property-tax comparison.

Your own bill can change differently because of property assessment, location, tax class and applicable levies. Water, wastewater, stormwater, garbage fees and provincial education taxes are outside this municipal increase comparison. These figures do not measure growth in the City’s total tax revenue.

Sources & method

What is being compared?

Each budget year’s published overall municipal property-tax increase is compared with the upper end of the original campaign range, 2.5%. The gap equals adopted increase minus 2.5, in percentage points. No inflation adjustment or cumulative household bill estimate is applied.

The 2022 budget predates this term and is excluded. The four annual budgets shown are 2023–2026. Council approves budgets; this page evaluates the campaign commitment without attributing every budget decision solely to the mayor.

Evidence limits and review process

The campaign benchmark comes from reporting published during the 2022 campaign; an archived original platform has not yet been obtained. The campaign article’s opening paragraph contains a conflicting number, while its headline and detailed framework agree on 2–2.5% and distinguish the later target.

2023 adoption is confirmed by CBC’s retrospective review and cross-checked against the City’s draft. The 2024 and 2025 adoption figures are supported by contemporary councillor accounts; 2026 uses the City’s adoption announcement. These are manually reviewed sources, not an automatic feed or independently audited household tax bills. No 2026 reelection promises are included.
