Fare methodology and evidence limits

Annual increase = (current price / previous price − 1) × 100. Cumulative increase = (current price / late-2022 price − 1) × 100. This equals compounding the exact annual price changes. No 2022 annual increase is inferred; no earlier continuous fare history is asserted.

The fare schedule is not a rider’s total spending. Free-service periods, transfers, fare caps, changes in payment availability and number of trips are not modelled. Adult card means the published card/e-purse fare, without implying every card payment method was available throughout the period. Monthly passes use the year they are valid, even when sold the preceding December.

This first comparison covers standard adult fares. Seniors, youth, EquiPass, Community Pass, Access Pass, U-Pass and Para Transpo products need separate review. The adult series is not a comprehensive audit of every fare category. 03 · Transit service

Bus reliability

The 2022 commitment

Improve bus service reliability and modernize routing and schedules for post-pandemic travel.

Paraphrase of the September 22, 2022 transportation platform coverage. Read the source ↗

The available annual KPIs show no sustained improvement. Service delivered fell from 97.8% in 2023 to 97% in 2025; punctuality fell from 74.5% to 72%. Frequent-route regularity was 82% in both 2024 and 2025.

This is a performance comparison, not a pass/fail verdict on the whole transit promise. The 2022 commitment did not specify a numerical threshold. These KPIs do not establish the effect of the mayor’s decisions or assess the routing changes themselves.

Definitions and targets
  • Service delivered: Share of scheduled bus trips that operated. This does not measure punctuality or the amount of service scheduled.
  • Punctuality · less-frequent routes: Departures at major stops between 1 minute early and 5 minutes late, on routes running every 16 minutes or less often. The archive does not provide a consistent early/late split.
  • Regularity · frequent routes: Share meeting the official spacing measure on frequent routes. This measures headway regularity, not whether each departure matched its timetable.

Methodology and limits

Reuses the existing reconciled official KPI series. Latest-publication selection and precision/revision handling follow the existing extraction methodology; conflicting source values remain in the export’s other_values field. No input observations or collector files are changed. The input SHA-256 is retained for reproducibility.

Monthly time gaps are not connected and no annual or year-to-date values are reconstructed from incomplete months. Pandemic-era punctuality observations from 2020–21 remain on the full KPI page; they are not a clean 2022 baseline. The scorecard and monthly file can differ in precision; monthly averages do not replace published annual results. The snapshot source_file column uses a legacy .pdf suffix for spreadsheet extracts; links here resolve to the actual committed .xlsx, with its worksheet identified.

Live GTFS predictions are excluded because their sampling, early-departure handling and definitions differ from official KPIs. The archive does not support a consistent early/on-time/late split for this period. Service delivered is a percentage of the timetable, not service hours, route coverage or customer satisfaction. Regularity is a distinct measure on frequent routes.

Sources & method

What is being compared?

For 2023–2026, each budget year’s published overall municipal property-tax increase is compared with the upper end of the original campaign range, 2.5%. The gap equals adopted increase minus 2.5, in percentage points. The 2013–2022 bars are historical context and receive no promise assessment.

Inflation = (annual-average CPI / previous annual-average CPI − 1) × 100. For the incomplete 2026 year, both averages use January–September. Inflation is rounded to one decimal in tax chart titles and the tax table; the CSV retains six decimals. Fare changes use two decimals in the display. No inflation adjustment or cumulative household bill estimate is applied. Same-year inflation was not fully known when budgets were adopted.

Council approves budgets; this page evaluates the campaign commitment without attributing every budget decision solely to the mayor. Historical rates follow the City’s own published comparison series; individual bills and separate fees can differ. Transit-fare methodology and source notes are in the fare section.

Evidence limits and review process

The campaign benchmark comes from reporting published during the 2022 campaign; an archived original platform has not yet been obtained. The campaign article’s opening paragraph contains a conflicting number, while its headline and detailed framework agree on 2–2.5% and distinguish the later target.

2013–2023 tax increases come from the City’s historical comparison table. The 2024 and 2025 adoption figures are supported by contemporary councillor accounts; 2026 uses the City’s adoption announcement. CPI is retrieved from Statistics Canada tables 18-10-0005-01 and 18-10-0004-01; inputs and response hashes are retained. These are reviewed snapshots, not an automatic feed or independently audited household tax bills. No 2026 reelection promises are included.
