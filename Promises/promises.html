01 · Affordability

Property-tax increases

The 2022 commitment

Limit increases to 2–2.5% in 2023 and 2024; target the same range in 2025 and 2026.

Paraphrase of the October 5, 2022 financial framework. Read the campaign coverage ↗

The 2023–24 commitment was met. Both years came in at 2.5%. Increases of 3.9% in 2025 and 3.75% in 2026 exceeded the later-year target.

These are the published overall municipal increases in Council-approved budgets. The comparison preserves the distinction between a firm commitment and a later target.

Sources & method

What is being compared?

For 2023–2026, each budget year’s published overall municipal property-tax increase is compared with the upper end of the original campaign range, 2.5%. The gap equals adopted increase minus 2.5, in percentage points. The 2013–2022 bars are historical context and receive no promise assessment.

Inflation = (annual-average CPI / previous annual-average CPI − 1) × 100. For the incomplete 2026 year, both averages use January–September. Inflation is rounded to one decimal in chart titles and the table; the CSV retains six decimals. No inflation adjustment or cumulative household bill estimate is applied. Same-year inflation was not fully known when budgets were adopted.

Council approves budgets; this page evaluates the campaign commitment without attributing every budget decision solely to the mayor. Historical rates follow the City’s own published comparison series; individual bills and separate fees can differ.

Evidence limits and review process

The campaign benchmark comes from reporting published during the 2022 campaign; an archived original platform has not yet been obtained. The campaign article’s opening paragraph contains a conflicting number, while its headline and detailed framework agree on 2–2.5% and distinguish the later target.

2013–2023 tax increases come from the City’s historical comparison table. The 2024 and 2025 adoption figures are supported by contemporary councillor accounts; 2026 uses the City’s adoption announcement. CPI is retrieved from Statistics Canada tables 18-10-0005-01 and 18-10-0004-01; inputs and response hashes are retained. These are reviewed snapshots, not an automatic feed or independently audited household tax bills. No 2026 reelection promises are included.
