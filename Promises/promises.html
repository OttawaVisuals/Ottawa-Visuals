01 · Affordability

Property-tax increases

The 2022 commitment

Limit increases to 2–2.5% in 2023 and 2024; target the same range in 2025 and 2026.

Paraphrase of the October 5, 2022 financial framework. Read the campaign coverage ↗

The 2023–24 commitment was met. Both years came in at 2.5%. Increases of 3.9% in 2025 and 3.75% in 2026 exceeded the later-year target.

These are the published overall municipal increases in Council-approved budgets. The comparison preserves the distinction between a firm commitment and a later target.

Fare methodology and evidence limits

Annual increase = (current price / previous price − 1) × 100. Cumulative increase = (current price / late-2022 price − 1) × 100. This equals compounding the exact annual price changes. No 2022 annual increase is inferred; no earlier continuous fare history is asserted.

The fare schedule is not a rider’s total spending. Free-service periods, transfers, fare caps, changes in payment availability and number of trips are not modelled. Adult card means the published card/e-purse fare, without implying every card payment method was available throughout the period. Monthly passes use the year they are valid, even when sold the preceding December.

This first comparison covers standard adult fares. Seniors, youth, EquiPass, Community Pass, Access Pass, U-Pass and Para Transpo products need separate review. The adult series is not a comprehensive audit of every fare category. Sources & method

What is being compared?

For 2023–2026, each budget year’s published overall municipal property-tax increase is compared with the upper end of the original campaign range, 2.5%. The gap equals adopted increase minus 2.5, in percentage points. The 2013–2022 bars are historical context and receive no promise assessment.

Inflation = (annual-average CPI / previous annual-average CPI − 1) × 100. For the incomplete 2026 year, both averages use January–September. Inflation is rounded to one decimal in tax chart titles and the tax table; the CSV retains six decimals. Fare changes use two decimals in the display. No inflation adjustment or cumulative household bill estimate is applied. Same-year inflation was not fully known when budgets were adopted.

Council approves budgets; this page evaluates the campaign commitment without attributing every budget decision solely to the mayor. Historical rates follow the City’s own published comparison series; individual bills and separate fees can differ. Transit-fare methodology and source notes are in the fare section.

Evidence limits and review process

The campaign benchmark comes from reporting published during the 2022 campaign; an archived original platform has not yet been obtained. The campaign article’s opening paragraph contains a conflicting number, while its headline and detailed framework agree on 2–2.5% and distinguish the later target.

2013–2023 tax increases come from the City’s historical comparison table. The 2024 and 2025 adoption figures are supported by contemporary councillor accounts; 2026 uses the City’s adoption announcement. CPI is retrieved from Statistics Canada tables 18-10-0005-01 and 18-10-0004-01; inputs and response hashes are retained. These are reviewed snapshots, not an automatic feed or independently audited household tax bills. No 2026 reelection promises are included.
