Coverage, definitions and missing data
Coverage of the data used on this page
SourceAvailable hereLimit
  • Police stops are records, not unique drivers. “Charged” counts stops that resulted in a charge, not the number of charges. “Final” stays under its source label: it is not assumed to mean no action. Data-quality records remain visible.
  • Annual, monthly, area and demographic police tables describe the same stops. They are checked against one another, never added together.
  • Demographic percentages divide a category’s stop count by all stops in the selected period and outcome, including unknown categories. These are recorded police categories, not population-adjusted stop rates or evidence of individual behaviour.
  • Collision charts count collision records by severity. Fatal collisions differ from people killed. Parties involved counts recorded vehicles or road users, not injuries. Blank party counts are not included in the sums.
  • Ward assignment for detailed collisions uses the existing Geo_ID join to 2022–2026 ward boundaries. Unmatched records remain an explicit category. Police neighbourhoods are source categories, not wards.
  • Camera totals count reported violations, not unique drivers. A camera can monitor one approach. “Per reporting camera” divides that month’s violations by camera approaches/sites with a published numeric value, including zero; it is not a traffic-volume rate.
  • A missing camera-month stays missing. It may reflect no published value or a camera not yet operating. Latest calendar years can be incomplete. Growing camera networks and changing traffic volumes affect totals.
  • OPS summaries use a separate reporting system and publication cutoff. They are kept out of the detailed City time series. The 2026 comparison uses the same September 19 cutoff in both years.
  • Speed-camera enforcement ended November 14, 2025. November is a shortened operating month. Red-light cameras were not affected. City announcement.
