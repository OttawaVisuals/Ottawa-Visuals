Ottawa RTO Watch

On Monday July 6, 2026 the federal public service went to four days a week in the office. Ottawa is a company town, so that is a city-scale change to how a few hundred thousand people move. This page measures what actually happened — commutes, transit, downtown parking, complaints, electricity — and is equally explicit about what the available data cannot yet show.

1 · The mandate

policy timeline

Two step changes bracket everything on this page: RTO3 in September 2024 and RTO4 in July 2026. Where a dataset has enough history, both are marked on the chart.

    2 · Commutes

    TomTom Routing / Traffic Flow / Incidents · logged on a Raspberry Pi

    Door-to-door driving time from five suburbs to downtown, both directions, sampled every 15 minutes at peak and hourly otherwise. This is our own measurement: TomTom's live map keeps no history, so these numbers exist only because we log them.

    Morning peak, day by day

    Mean AM-peak (06:30–09:30) driving time to downtown, per weekday. Weekends excluded — they are not commutes.

    City-wide congestion, day by day

    Peak congestion is the share below free-flow speed across 15 fixed road points.

    Active traffic jams — today

    The baseline problem. Our traffic collection began Jul 6, 2026 — the day RTO4 took effect. So this section can show the phase-in (Jul 6 → Sep 15) but has no pre-RTO4 "before" of its own. The only genuine before/after is the TomTom MOVE comparison of August 2024 against August 2026, which is why that August window is treated as schedule-critical.

    3 · Transit

    OC Transpo GTFS-Realtime · buses only (the feed excludes the O-Train)

    If a mandate forces demand onto transit, does service keep up? We poll OC Transpo's realtime feed every five minutes. The feed publishes predicted arrival times but no delay fields, so the honest live metrics are fleet size, cancellations and speed — not on-time performance.

    Fleet and cancellations, day by day

    Buses at AM peak and PM peak, with the mean share of tracked trips cancelled. Weekends excluded.

    Buses on the road — today

    Official figures, archived. OC Transpo's four KPI spreadsheets (ridership, on-time performance, service delivery, fleet health) are published as rolling ~13-month windows — old months silently drop off. We snapshot them so the history survives; the archive lives in OC_Transpo/rt_data/kpi_snapshots/.

    4 · Downtown parking

    City of Ottawa parking feed · no API key

    Congestion says people are moving. A full downtown garage says they arrived and stayed — which makes garage occupancy the most direct office-occupancy proxy we can get. Like the traffic feed it publishes no history, so this series starts the day we began logging it.

    Downtown garage occupancy — today

    Coverage. The feed lists 15 municipal lots but only five publish live space counts — four of them downtown (Gloucester, both Clarence garages, 110 Laurier), roughly 1,800 spaces. That is the whole basis of the downtown figure. Park & Ride lots publish capacity but not occupancy, so commuter-lot fill cannot be tracked this way.

    5 · The city responds

    311 · IESO · bicycle counters

    Three independent series that should move if a few hundred thousand commuting patterns change. Unlike our own collection, two of these have real history — so they can be read against 2025 as well as against Jul 6.

    311 service requests, weekly

    Requests opened per week. These are complaints, not conditions — volume tracks reporting behaviour as much as road state. The final partial week is dropped.

    Weekday vs weekend electricity load, Ottawa zone

    Office-hours (09:00–18:00) load. Absolute MW is dominated by weather, so the number to read is the weekday ÷ weekend ratio — weather hits both, occupancy only the weekday. Whole IESO zone, not downtown.

    6 · The City's own counts

    Open Ottawa · annual

    The City surveys traffic volumes at hundreds of intersections and midblock segments. This is the authoritative measure of how much traffic Ottawa carries — and it is published annually, latest edition 2024. It establishes the level RTO4 landed on; it cannot yet show RTO4 itself.

    Surveyed intersection volumes by edition

    AADT of all motorized vehicles across surveyed intersections. The site list changes between editions, so median per site is the comparable column — not the total.

    What a real step change looks like

    COVID-19 intersection monitoring, 2020–22

    The City measured 2020 volumes as a percentage of normal. This is the yardstick: a genuine collapse in Ottawa commuting took the AM peak to roughly a third of normal. It sets the scale against which any RTO4 effect should be judged — and reminds us that RTO4 moves one day a week, not a whole workforce.

    7 · Emissions

    City of Ottawa GHG inventory · annual

    More driving means more emissions, and Ottawa publishes a greenhouse-gas inventory. But read the granularity first: it is annual and city-wide, and the latest inventory year is 2024 — eighteen months before RTO4. It cannot measure the mandate. What it can show is the trend the mandate landed on.

    Community emissions by sector

    Tonnes CO₂e. Transportation had already recovered past its 2012 level by 2024, while buildings stayed below theirs.

    What one extra commuting day costs, per 10,000 drivers

    This is an arithmetic estimate, not a measurement. It is expressed per 10,000 additional daily car round-trips precisely so it carries no assumption about how many public servants there are or how they travel — scale it yourself. Distance comes from the corridor lengths we actually measure; the emission factor is a stated assumption.

    8 · Methodology & limits

    every source, cadence and caveat

    This repository is the methodology. Each dataset below names what it measures, how often it updates, and what it cannot tell you. Where a series cannot speak to RTO4, that is stated rather than hidden behind a chart.

    What we collect ourselves
    TomTom corridor commute times · every 15 min at peak, hourly otherwise
    Five suburb↔downtown corridors, both directions, via the Routing API with live traffic. Logged since Jul 6, 2026. Limit: starts the day RTO4 took effect — no self-collected "before".
    TomTom segment speeds & incidents · same cadence
    15 fixed road points (current vs free-flow speed) and a bounding-box incident scan. Limit: a congestion proxy, not a volume count.
    OC Transpo GTFS-Realtime · every 5 min
    Vehicle positions and trip updates, logged since Jul 11, 2026. Limits: the feed is bus-only (no O-Train) and carries no delay fields, so on-time performance must be computed offline against the static GTFS rather than read off the feed.
    Municipal parking occupancy · weekday daytime every 15 min, hourly otherwise
    City parking feed, no key required. Limit: only 5 of 15 lots publish live space counts; the downtown figure rests on four garages (~1,800 spaces).
    OC Transpo KPI spreadsheets · weekly snapshot
    Four official files, each a rolling ~13-month window. Snapshotted because a missed week is lost permanently.
    External datasets, and exactly what each one can prove
    Ottawa 311 service requests · daily source, refreshed weekly
    Current-year and previous-year CSVs (~35 MB each), aggregated to weekly counts by service area and ward. Strength: the previous-year file gives a genuine 2025 baseline for the same calendar weeks — rare here. Limits: complaints, not conditions; date-only resolution; the newest week is always incomplete and is dropped.
    IESO Ottawa-zone hourly demand · weekly
    Zonal demand published since 2003, so it spans RTO3 and RTO4. Limits: whole zone, not downtown, and dominated by weather — which is why the page reads the weekday÷weekend ratio rather than the MW level.
    Bicycle trip counters · monthly re-check
    Cannot speak to RTO4. Verified 2026-07-25: coverage ends Mar 31, 2026, and only the Adàwe counters still report in 2026. Shown as a pre-RTO4 baseline on the one continuous counter, with the cross-year total deliberately not charted because the reporting counter set shrinks each year.
    Intersection & midblock volumes · annual
    Latest edition 2024; no 2025 or 2026 edition exists yet. Cannot speak to RTO4. Field names differ between vintages, so editions are mapped individually. Compare median-per-site, never totals — the surveyed site list changes every edition.
    COVID-19 intersection monitoring · 2020–22, closed series
    Values are percentages of normal volume, not counts. Included as the yardstick for what a large, real change in Ottawa commuting looks like in the City's own data.
    Collisions · annual
    2017–2024, and 2023 is missing from the source. The 2024 injury total also falls far below its neighbours, which points at incomplete coding rather than a real drop — so injuries are not charted. Collision counts are context, not an RTO4 effect.
    City of Ottawa GHG inventory · annual
    Annual, city-wide, latest year 2024. Cannot speak to RTO4. Used for trend context, plus an explicitly-labelled arithmetic estimate expressed as a rate.
    Datasets checked and rejected

    Recorded so the absences are deliberate rather than accidental:

    Park & Ride occupancy
    The feed publishes capacity but no occupancy field (verified 2026-07-25). Commuter-lot fill would have been an excellent RTO4 indicator; it is simply not published.
    Ottawa GHG dashboard, monthly or geographic breakdown
    Does not exist. The inventory is annual and city-wide only.
    Traffic Services webcam images
    Require a certificate string per request, and images would need vehicle counting to become data. Out of scope.

    Still outstanding and genuinely valuable: TBS federal headcount for the National Capital Region (the denominator — how many people RTO4 actually moves), StatCan work-from-home rates by CMA (the compliance curve), and downtown office vacancy from commercial brokerages (manual transcription).

    Reproducing this page

    Live feeds are polled continuously on a Raspberry Pi (Traffic/scripts/, OC_Transpo/scripts/) and pushed hourly. External datasets are refreshed with python RTO4/scripts/refresh_all.py, which writes the small aggregate JSON files in RTO4/data/ that this page loads. Every fetcher is stdlib-only and documents its own caveats at the top of the file.

    The banner at the top of this page compares the newest reading to the current time and warns if collection has stalled — so old numbers are never presented as live.
